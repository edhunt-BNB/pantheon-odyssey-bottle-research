Glass & Bottle Sourcing

Supplier analysis for the Odyssey Rum launch bottle

This report evaluates two candidate European glass manufacturers for Odyssey Rum's launch bottle — Stoelzle Glass Group (Polish operation) and Vetreria Etrusca (Italy) — against an Art Deco specification, with full cost, logistics, and tooling analysis. A break-even model compares the full-custom mould path against the faster option of modifying an existing bottle from supplier stock.

Prepared for Pantheon Spirits Ltd Subject Odyssey Rum · 700ml · Flint · T-Cork First Order 1,000 bottles Destination Roudham, Norfolk (NR16)
Executive Summary

The bottle is the brand.

For a rum brand whose internal mandate is to "Make Rum Sexy Again," the glass carries as much weight as what's inside it. This report is the sourcing spine of that decision.

Odyssey Rum's launch bottle must achieve three things simultaneously: read as distinctly Art Deco on the back bar, pass quality scrutiny at a premium retail tier, and land at a cost that preserves the brand's 16–20% wholesale margin. Those three requirements are in tension with each other — a fully bespoke Art Deco mould satisfies the first, the second, and fails the third at 1,000-bottle order volumes. Resolving that tension is what this document is about.

We assessed two shortlisted European glass manufacturers, Stoelzle Glass Group (Polish operation at Częstochowa) and Vetreria Etrusca (Montelupo Fiorentino, Tuscany). A gate-check on the broader European market added two further contenders that genuinely merit consideration: Saverglass (France) for its far deeper Art Deco stock catalogue, and Verallia UK (Leeds) for the structural advantage of domestic UK shipping post-Brexit. We explicitly considered — and eliminated — eight other candidates.

The central question

Should Odyssey commission a full custom mould to realise the exact ideal bottle design (ribbed sides, flat front panel, thick wavy base, short-neck T-cork finish), or modify an existing supplier bottle — most realistically Vetreria Etrusca's Aurea Spirits 700ml bottle (launched 2025) — to get within ~80% of the brief at a fraction of the tooling cost?

Note: Vetreria's Monet Deco (the design the brief originally anchored on) is a 500ml-only bottle and does not fit Odyssey's 70cl launch volume. It does, however, become an excellent vehicle for a future 50cl limited-edition SKU.

The break-even maths in the Cost Breakdown tab answers this in pounds and pence. Short version: at a 12,000-bottle annual run-rate and a five-year mould life, the delta between the two paths is roughly £0.23 per bottle. Whether that delta is worth absorbing depends on how much incremental retail velocity a fully bespoke bottle drives versus a modified stock bottle dressed in Odyssey's label design.

Early read — subject to supplier quotes

The likely right call: Vetreria Etrusca, modified Aurea Spirits for launch

Vetreria's Aurea Spirits bottle (700ml, Extra Flint, bar-top) is a purpose-designed premium spirits bottle launched in 2025 — the right capacity, the right closure, the right glass grade. Adding vertical rib embossing and a thickened wavy base via mould modification is a realistic £3k–£6k tooling spend, versus £18k–£25k for a fully custom Stoelzle mould. At 12,000 bottles/year, the bespoke premium is ~£0.23/bottle over a five-year mould life — real money for a startup.

Approach Vetreria with a two-bottle commercial proposal. Aurea 700ml as the Odyssey launch bottle, Monet Deco 500ml as a Year-2 limited-edition line extension. Doubling the conversation doubles your negotiating leverage.

Stoelzle is the right partner when you hit 50,000+ bottles/year and are ready to capitalise a bespoke mould. Saverglass is the backup if Vetreria can't deliver clean modifications. Verallia UK is the "break glass in case of Brexit emergency" option.

This is an early read based on desk research — the real call will be made after both primaries quote. Next step: send the brief to both.

The Art Deco question

NELSON and ICE — closest Standard Collection matches

Stoelzle's Standard Collection (stoelzlespirits.com/standard-collection-range) has ten 700ml stock bottles. Two are plausible Art Deco starting points for Odyssey:

NELSON — 750g, 227.7mm tall, Ø86.1mm, conical neck with groove and ring. This is the strongest Deco silhouette in Stoelzle's catalogue. Read-through Art Deco could be achieved via acid-etch frosting on the sides (creating the visual of vertical ribs without new tooling) plus embossed branding on the front panel. Getting you probably 70% of the brief at zero mould cost.

ICE — 750g, 196.7mm tall, 83.9×83.9mm square form, angular shoulders, vintage ring. Square-form proxy for the ideal's rectangular profile. Again, decoration layer rather than new mould.

Neither is a perfect match to the ideal, but both clear the quality bar and eliminate tooling cost — turning Stoelzle from "premium supplier requiring 10k+ MOQ" into "viable at 1,000 units."

The Art Deco question — key finding

Aurea Spirits is the right starting point for Odyssey, not Monet Deco

Monet Deco is 500ml only — verified on their live catalogue. It's a strong Art Deco silhouette, but at 500ml it doesn't fit Odyssey's 700ml spec. Scaling a bottle from 500ml to 700ml requires a new parent mould, not a modification — so the "low-tweak" economics are narrower than initially assumed.

Aurea Spirits (launched 2025) is available in both 500ml and 700ml, with bar-top cork or GPI screw finish, in Extra Flint. Built around the golden-ratio proportion. This is the bottle to modify for Odyssey — it gives you the right volume, the right closure, and the right glass grade as a starting point. Add vertical rib embossing and a thickened wavy base and it reads unmistakably Art Deco.

Monet Deco at 500ml is a strategic asset for a limited-edition SKU. Odyssey's Year 2 could include a 50cl "Navigator's Edition" or "Cask Strength" release in the Monet Deco bottle — no new mould work, a reason to return to Vetreria, and it visually differentiates the line extension on the back bar from the 70cl flagship.

Revised recommendationApproach Vetreria with two bottles in the conversation, not one: Aurea Spirits 700ml as the Odyssey launch bottle (base for modification to add ribs + wave base), and Monet Deco 500ml as a future line extension. This doubles the size of the commercial relationship you're offering them, which materially improves your negotiating position.

The single most important analytical question in this report: is the fully custom Stoelzle mould worth the additional £13,500 in tooling cost vs. modifying Vetreria Etrusca's existing Aurea Spirits 700ml? The answer depends on (a) how many bottles Odyssey actually sells and (b) how much incremental wholesale price a bespoke design commands.

Break-even at current assumptions

Your cost model assumes 1,000 bottles/month, i.e. 12,000 per year. A glass mould typically produces for 5–8 years. Taking 5 years as the conservative end, that's 60,000 bottles over the mould's working life.

  • Vetreria modified mould: £4,500 tooling ÷ 60,000 bottles = £0.075/bottle
  • Stoelzle custom mould: £18,000 tooling ÷ 60,000 bottles = £0.300/bottle
  • Bespoke-design premium: £0.23/bottle over 60k units = £13,500 total capital outlay

At what volume does the bespoke premium become trivial?

The custom mould's amortised cost falls below £0.10/bottle (effectively invisible in a £28 wholesale) at 180,000 bottles of production. That's 30,000 bottles/year over 6 years, or 15,000/year over 12 years.

What breaks the decision: if a bespoke bottle commands even £0.50 more wholesale (£45.50 RRP instead of £45.00), the £13,500 bespoke premium is recovered in 27,000 bottles — inside the mould's first lifetime. If bespoke drives £1 more wholesale, payback is 13,500 bottles. If it drives no price lift at all, the modified path is obviously cheaper.

The decision framework

Start with Vetreria modified, plan for Stoelzle at Year 3

Launching with a modified Aurea Spirits 700ml protects £13,500 of capital during the riskiest phase of the business (launch + first 18 months of proving demand). If Odyssey scales to 30,000+ bottles/year, commission a fully bespoke Stoelzle mould for a relaunch SKU at Year 3 — by then the brand has the cash flow and the data on which design elements matter most to the retail buyer.

If the Aurea modification doesn't deliver enough bespoke feel to support the £45 RRP positioning, the fall-back is Saverglass's SUPREME decanter — no mould cost at all — at a £0.80–£1.20/bottle unit price premium. Either way, Stoelzle's Standard Collection NELSON or ICE with acid-etch frosting remains a third viable route at low volume if decorative substitution can stand in for structural embossing.

Choosing Incoterms

For the first order, FCA (Free Carrier) is the recommended Incoterm from both suppliers. This gives Pantheon control of the freight (meaning you choose the carrier, negotiate the rate, and retain visibility) while the supplier handles EU export clearance at their factory. Alternative options:

  • EXW (Ex-Works) — cheapest on paper but Pantheon becomes responsible for EU export paperwork, which most UK importers are not set up to handle.
  • DAP (Delivered at Place) — supplier handles freight to NR16. Simpler but typically 10–20% more expensive because the supplier's forwarder margin is baked in. Worth considering for the first order to de-risk.
  • DDP (Delivered Duty Paid) — fully door-to-door including UK import. Not recommended — you lose visibility on VAT recovery and the supplier's UK customs agent fee is usually a poor deal.

UK import regulatory framework

Empty glass bottles are among the lightest regulatory imports into the UK. The entire regulatory stack that applies to Odyssey's glass procurement is summarised below — there are no hidden surprises, but a few items Pantheon must have in place before the first order.

Tariff classification and duties

  • HS code: 7010904300 — "Colourless glass bottles, beverages, 0.33L–1L capacity"
  • UK Import Duty rate: 0.00% under the UK Global Tariff (confirmed on UK Integrated Online Tariff, April 2026)
  • Trade agreement: UK-EU Trade & Cooperation Agreement applies. Origin rules are relevant but irrelevant in practice here because the duty rate is zero regardless of origin.
  • Import VAT: 20% of customs value. Set up Postponed VAT Accounting (PVA) before the first shipment — this means no VAT is paid at the border (it's instead declared and reclaimed on the quarterly VAT return, so cash-flow neutral). Free to set up via HMRC.

Food Contact Materials compliance

Glass is the lowest-risk food contact material in the UK regulatory framework (glass migration is essentially zero). The compliance obligation is:

  • Obtain a Declaration of Compliance (DoC) from each supplier confirming the bottles meet UK-retained Regulation (EC) No 1935/2004 on materials intended to come into contact with food, as implemented by The Materials and Articles in Contact with Food (England) Regulations 2012 (SI 2012/2619).
  • The DoC is not submitted to any government authority. Keep it on company records for the life of the product, available if Trading Standards request it.
  • All tier-1 European glassworks issue this document routinely. Both Stoelzle and Vetreria Etrusca will produce one on request.

UKCA and CE marking

Not required for empty glass bottles. Glass food-contact packaging is outside both the UKCA product safety regime and CE marking requirements. Save the compliance budget for the label (which does have product-information regulatory requirements when filled — see below).

When the bottles are filled — flagged for later

This report is about empty glass, but for reference when Odyssey is filled, bottled, and sold:

  • UK Excise Duty: £33.99/LPA from 1 Feb 2026 → ~£10.00 per 700ml bottle at 42% ABV
  • AWRS (Alcohol Wholesaler Registration Scheme) required for wholesale movement
  • Label must carry UK FBO address, allergen info, alcohol strength, net volume, duty-paid text
  • Recycling mark (e.g. "Glass — Widely Recycled") optional but standard practice

None of these apply to the empty-glass import itself.

Border Target Operating Model (BTOM)

Empty glass is classified as BTOM Category 1 — Low Risk Dry Goods. Operational implications:

  • No phytosanitary certificate
  • No veterinary certificate
  • No IPAFFS pre-notification
  • No Border Control Post inspection routing
  • Typical physical inspection rate: 2–5% (random, at destination in many cases)
  • Required: Entry Summary Declaration (ENS) filed by carrier 2 hours before arrival · Goods Movement Reference (GMR) for RoRo · Commercial invoice + packing list + CMR consignment note

Documents needed before first shipment

  • GB EORI number (gov.uk/eori · free · ~3 days)
  • Registered UK VAT number + PVA set up in HMRC account
  • UK customs broker appointed (optional but recommended for first 2–3 shipments — budget £60–150/shipment)
  • Supplier DoC (Declaration of Compliance) on file for each SKU
  • Supplier's EORI (EU) — they should already have this if they export regularly
  • Packing list matching commercial invoice exactly
  • CMR consignment note (prepared by carrier)

Commercial wins at each tier

Beyond the unit price, bigger volume unlocks softer commercial benefits that matter to cash flow and launch logistics:

  • 5,000 bottles: Extended payment terms (30 days net from invoice instead of proforma). One-off waiver of a small mould-modification fee.
  • 10,000+ bottles: Guaranteed production slot windows (useful for launch planning). Free or subsidised first-order sample run (100-200 bottles). Priority queuing for re-orders.
  • 25,000+ bottles: Multi-cavity mould possible (halves per-unit production cost on subsequent runs). Dedicated account manager. Annual price lock for stability.
  • 50,000+ bottles: Mould capitalised by supplier (they absorb tooling cost and build it into unit price over the contract). Warehouse-and-call-off arrangement (supplier holds stock, Pantheon pays on draw-down — big cash flow benefit). Ability to co-develop new bottles without upfront tooling spend.
Negotiation lever

Frame the first order as a launch tranche

Neither supplier will offer their best rates for 1,000 bottles in isolation. The right play is to open with: "Our launch tranche is 1,000 bottles in [month], followed by a projected 10,000 in the first 12 months and 25,000 in the second year. We're evaluating you against [other supplier] on total relationship value, not single-order price." Both will improve their offer materially if they believe the volume is coming.

Pre-launch import readiness checklist

Everything Pantheon needs in place before the first shipment of glass arrives at NR16. Most items are free and administrative. Budget 4–6 weeks lead time to get all of this in place alongside the supplier qualification process.

  • Obtain GB EORI number from HMRC (gov.uk/eori — free, ~3 working days)
  • Register for UK VAT (if not already) — required for PVA
  • Activate Postponed VAT Accounting in HMRC online account (2 minutes)
  • Choose and brief a UK customs broker for the first 2–3 shipments
  • Appoint a UK freight forwarder for both Poland and Italy lanes
  • Request Declaration of Compliance (Reg 1935/2004) from supplier, signed and on letterhead
  • Confirm supplier's EU EORI and VAT export status
  • Agree Incoterms in writing on the commercial agreement (recommend FCA)
  • Confirm pallet specification with supplier (Euro pallet, heat-treated where applicable)
  • Confirm carton specification with supplier (6-bottle, separator inserts, double-wall corrugated)
  • Receive sample bottles (20–50) for co-packer trial fill and closure fit test — BEFORE placing first production order
  • Co-packer confirms compatibility with their bottling line (neck finish tolerance, carton orientation for infeed)
  • Product liability insurance includes container failure scenarios (standard PLI does)
  • Record-keeping system in place for 5-year DoC and batch traceability

Ongoing cost efficiency moves

  • Consolidate orders. A single 5,000-bottle order is 35–40% cheaper on freight than five 1,000-bottle orders. Even if warehousing costs Pantheon £500/year to hold inventory, the freight savings dwarf that from order 2 onwards.
  • Consider a Duty Deferment Account (DDA) once doing 6+ declarations per year — worth the overhead from that point. Not before.
  • Explore pooled freight with other UK craft spirits brands — Pantheon's Norfolk co-packer likely receives glass from 3–5 different brands; consolidating inbound shipments across those brands could halve per-pallet freight cost.
  • Warehouse-at-source at Stoelzle or Vetreria (supplier holds your stock for a small storage fee) — once annual volume exceeds ~15,000 bottles, this can let Pantheon draw down monthly without the cash-flow shock of 100% upfront purchase.
